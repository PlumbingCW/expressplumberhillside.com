Basement waterproofing in Hillside starts with finding where the water comes from. It might be groundwater through the walls, a leaking pipe, a failed sump or sewage coming up the floor drain. The fix is different for each, and treating the wrong one wastes money.

Interior drainage systems

An interior drain collects water at the base of the wall and carries it to a sump pit. The pump then sends it out of the house. It is a practical fix for basements without exterior access, which is common in dense neighborhoods. See sump pump installation for the pump side.

Backwater valves and sewer backups

If your basement floods because sewage comes up the floor drain during heavy rain, a backwater valve can stop it. The valve closes when flow reverses. It needs a permit and maintenance. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 about the rules for your lot.

Downspouts, grading and drains

Many wet basements are helped by moving roof water away. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the foundation, and soil should slope away from the house. These are cheap fixes and often the first step.

Why older Hillside basements get wet

More than 79 percent of Hillside homes were built before 1960. Older foundations of block or stone are porous and have aging drain tile, if any. Many basements also hold the water heater, laundry and boiler, which makes a flood costly.

Two-family and rental basements

Many Hillside basements serve more than one household. A tenant may store belongings and run the laundry down there while the owner pays for the structure. A leak becomes a question of who calls and who approves the repair. We talk to both, explain the cause in plain terms and give the owner a written description of what we found.

If the basement holds a boiler or water heater, flooding is more than a nuisance. A wet burner can shut off heat for the whole building. That is one reason we recommend raising equipment on blocks and adding a sump alarm before the next storm.

After the water: drying and mold

Once a basement has been wet, dry it within a day or two. Open windows when the air outside is drier, run a dehumidifier and pull up wet carpet or cardboard. Mold needs moisture and time, and a wall that stays damp for a week is a mold risk. We fix the plumbing and drainage. Drying and mold cleanup are separate trades, and we will say so.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  • Did they find the source of the water, or only sell a product?
  • Will the work include a pump, a battery backup and a high water alarm?
  • Where will the discharge pipe end?
  • Is a permit needed and who pulls it?
  • What does the warranty cover in writing?

A contractor who skips the first question is selling a product, not a fix.
