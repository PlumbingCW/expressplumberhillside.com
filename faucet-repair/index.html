A faucet that sprays, will not shut off or leaks under the sink is a job for emergency faucet repair in Hillside. Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If they are stuck or missing, close the main water valve, then call the 24/7 line.

Most faucet repairs are small. A dripping spout is often a worn cartridge or seat and takes less time than the wait for the appointment. The emergency is when water is escaping somewhere it should not.

When a faucet leak is an emergency

  • A supply line spraying or dripping heavily under the sink.
  • A handle that will not turn off the water.
  • A leak that soaks the cabinet floor or runs into the ceiling below.
  • A broken faucet on a sink that is the only one in the home.

Kitchen faucets and sprayers

Kitchen faucets work the hardest. A pull-down sprayer that will not retract has a stuck weight or a kinked hose. A faucet that sags is loose under the sink. We open the cabinet, trace the leak from the top down and fix the cause, not only the drip.

Bathroom, tub and shower valves

A tub or shower valve that drips is a cartridge job. Left alone, it wastes water at every use and stains the fixture. Shower valves behind a wall may need access, and if the valve is badly corroded, replacement is better than repeated repairs.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

A hose bib that leaks inside the wall during winter is a quiet danger. Water can freeze in the pipe behind it and split the line. Disconnect hoses in autumn. A frost-free bib, which runs the shutoff inside the heated space, is the long term fix. If a bib is already leaking, see burst pipe repair.

Sinks, drains and supply lines

A sink that drains slowly is a trap or line problem. A sink that leaks around the drain flange needs the flange reseated. Braided supply lines last roughly a decade and fail without notice, so replacing old ones is cheap insurance. A kitchen sink with a disposal may also need garbage disposal service.

Repair or replace

A faucet in good shape with a bad cartridge is a repair. A faucet with a pitted finish, a leaking body or no available parts is a replacement. We tell you which, with the cost of each, so you decide.

Choosing a replacement faucet

If repair is not worth it, match the new faucet to the sink. Count the holes in the sink or counter, because a three hole sink needs a different faucet than a single hole one. Check the spout reach and height so water lands in the center of the basin. Solid brass bodies and ceramic cartridges outlast plastic parts.

For a kitchen, a pull down sprayer is convenient, but check the hose weight and the clearance under the sink. For a bathroom, a widespread faucet needs three openings. Bring the old faucet's model number and we will tell you whether parts still exist.

Shutoff valves are part of the repair

Old shutoff valves under a sink often leak or will not close. We replace them with quarter turn valves when we do the repair, so the next one is a five minute job. A faucet repair without working shutoffs means closing the main every time.

What affects the price

  • Repair of one part versus a new faucet.
  • Brand and availability of parts.
  • Access under the sink or behind the wall.
  • Condition of shutoff valves and supply lines.
  • Time of day.

Standards and sources behind this page

Faucets labeled under the EPA WaterSense program are tested against flow standards. The shutoffs and supply lines behind them follow the code in the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards.
