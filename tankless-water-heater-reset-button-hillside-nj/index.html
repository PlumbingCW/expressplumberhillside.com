Tankless Water Heater Reset Button for Hillside, NJ Homes

How to reset a tankless unit safely and what the error code is really telling you.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

Many tankless units have no single reset button. Most reset by turning the power off for a minute and back on, or by pressing a reset on the control panel. Before you reset anything, read the error code on the display. The code tells you why the unit shut down, and resetting without fixing the cause just brings the fault back.

If you smell gas, do not reset anything. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Step by step: power cycle

  1. Note the codeWrite down any error code or take a photo of the display.
  2. Turn off powerSwitch off the unit or the breaker for at least a minute.
  3. Check the basicsMake sure the gas valve is open and the vent is clear.
  4. Restore powerSwitch it back on and run a hot tap for a minute.
  5. Watch the resultIf the code returns, stop and call.

What the codes usually mean

  • Ignition codes often mean a gas supply problem or a dirty flame sensor.
  • Overheat codes point to scale or a failed sensor.
  • Flow codes suggest a clogged filter or a low flow rate.
  • Vent codes mean the exhaust or air intake is blocked.

Codes differ by brand, so check your manual. A plumber can read and clear them and tell you the cause.

When resetting is not enough

  • The code comes back within a day.
  • You smell gas or see soot near the unit.
  • Water leaks from the unit or the pipes.
  • The unit has not been flushed in over a year.
  • Water is lukewarm even after a reset.

A flush often clears scale. Our tankless water heater service page explains flushing and repair, and if the unit has failed for good see water heater installation.

Before you touch the unit: a short safety list

  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Do not remove the cover on a gas unit.
  • Do not reset an electric unit more than once without finding the cause.
  • Never bypass a safety switch.

Why a unit locks out

Tankless units are designed to shut down when a condition is unsafe, such as no flame, overheating or blocked venting. A lockout is the unit protecting itself. A reset restarts the sequence, and if the same condition exists, it will lock out again. That is why the cause matters more than the reset.

What to tell a technician

Give the brand, model, error code, how long it has been happening and what changed recently, such as a power outage or a water supply interruption. If a flush is overdue, say so. A technician can use those details to arrive with the right parts.

A simple log helps

Keep a note by the unit with the date, the code and what you did. If the same code returns three times in a month, you have a pattern, and a pattern means a repair is due. A log also helps a technician see whether the problem is getting worse or staying steady.

Power outages and tankless units

Even gas tankless units need electric power for the controls and the fan. When the power goes out, the unit stops. When power returns, it may display a code. Wait a minute and power cycle it. If it will not restart, check the condensate drain, the inlet filter and the vent, then call.

Winter care

A unit on an exterior wall or in an unheated space can freeze. Most have freeze protection that uses electricity, which does not help in an outage. If your unit is exposed, insulate the pipes and keep the space above freezing during a hard cold snap.

How often is too often?

An occasional reset after a power flicker is normal. Resetting weekly is not. A unit that needs frequent resets is warning you about something, such as a failing flame sensor, a clogged vent or heavy scale. Do not let the warning become routine.

Why flushing matters for resets

Scale narrows the heat exchanger and makes the unit overheat. A flush with a descaling solution clears it. We often see a unit that needed weekly resets run for months after a flush. If you have never flushed yours, this is the first step to try before replacing parts.

Prevent the next shutdown

  • Flush the unit at least once a year.
  • Clean the inlet filter every few months.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear.
  • Replace the condensate neutralizer media if your model has one.

Standards and sources behind this page

Manufacturers set the reset and lockout rules for each tankless model, and your manual is the final word. For gas units, the CDC carbon monoxide guidance explains why a lockout caused by blocked venting is not something to bypass.
