A sump pump works only when it is needed, usually during the worst storm. That is why failures show up at the worst moment.

When a sump pump failure is an emergency

  • Water in the pit rising and the pump not starting.
  • The pump runs but the water does not leave.
  • A power outage during heavy rain.
  • A pump that runs constantly and never catches up.
  • A loud grinding, rattling or burning smell.

Backup systems

Heavy storms often cause power failures. A battery backup pump or a water powered backup keeps the pit from overflowing when the main pump loses power. A high water alarm gives you a warning. Backup pumps need their own checks, since a dead battery is no help.

Installing a new sump pump

  1. Check the pitWe inspect the pit, check valve and discharge line.
  2. Set the new pumpThe pump is placed on a stable base and the float is set to clear the walls.
  3. Connect dischargeThe discharge line runs outside, away from the foundation, with a check valve.
  4. Test under loadWe fill the pit and watch the pump start, pump and shut off.

Where the water should go

A sump discharge must carry water away from the house, not onto a neighbor's property or into the sanitary sewer, which is not allowed in many places. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 about local rules for discharge.

Flood risk in the Hillside area

Heavy rain and flood events in New Jersey have put many basements at risk. Check FEMA flood maps for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage, because standard homeowners policies generally exclude it. See also basement waterproofing for long term water control.

How a sump system works

A sump system has a pit, a pump with a float switch, a check valve and a discharge line. Groundwater reaches the pit through drains or soil. When the water reaches the float, the pump starts and sends it out through the discharge pipe. The check valve keeps water from running back.

Sizing and discharge height

A pump has to lift water from the pit to the point where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and the higher it lifts, the less it moves. A pump that is fine on paper can fall short if the discharge climbs a long way. We size by the total lift and by how fast the pit fills during a storm.

A storm night checklist

  • Pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts.
  • Check that the discharge pipe is clear and not frozen.
  • Move stored items off the floor.
  • Charge or check the backup battery.
  • Keep our number on the fridge.
