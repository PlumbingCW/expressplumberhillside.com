Elizabeth is Union County's seat and a large, mixed city. Plumbing needs change from one neighborhood to the next, so we plan the visit around where you are.

Elizabeth neighborhood by neighborhood

  • Elmora and Elmora Hills: older single-family blocks where basement water heaters and boilers date from earlier decades.
  • Peterstown: dense rowhouses and two-family buildings, where one stack serves two apartments.
  • Bayway: many two- to four-family homes, so a clog in the basement can reach several kitchens.
  • North End: houses largely built in the 1920s, with original drain lines.
  • Midtown and Frog Hollow: older buildings, some with storefronts below apartments.
  • Elizabethport and the waterfront: older multifamily buildings and industrial properties near the river.

We use those differences. A call from Bayway usually means a shared drain, so we bring heavier cable gear. A call from an Elmora basement is more likely a heater or a sump.

Older buildings, multifamily lines

About 54 percent of Elizabeth's housing units were built before 1960, with more than a quarter dating to 1939 or earlier, per the Census Bureau's American Community Survey figures. That is a lot of cast iron and old supply pipe. In a multifamily building the cost of a failure multiplies, because one broken stack touches every unit it serves.

Water heaters and boilers in Elizabeth basements

Elizabeth's older buildings often run steam or hot water boilers beside a tank water heater. A cold morning can bring both calls at once. We handle water heater repair and boiler repair in the same visit when we can.

Sewer lines and the Elizabeth River

The Elizabeth River, about 4.2 miles long and largely channelized, drains through the city to the Arthur Kill. Low lying blocks near it see heavy flows in storms. A main line that is already partly blocked backs up first. Sewer line cleaning clears it, and a camera inspection shows why it clogged.

Getting here from Hillside

Routes 1 and 9, Route 27 and Route 439 all pass through the city, and the New Jersey Turnpike runs along its eastern side. Hillside is on the city's western edge, so most dispatches are short. We also cover Kenilworth and Roselle Park, just west, with the same number.

Permits for plumbing work in Elizabeth go through the city's construction office. Ask them before replacing a boiler, water heater or sewer line.

Water heaters and hard winter calls

Elizabeth's housing mix means winter calls are varied. A rowhouse in Peterstown may lose heat when a boiler stops, while an Elmora single-family loses hot water when a tank fails. We tell you honestly when a repair makes sense and when replacement is smarter.

If you manage a building in Elizabeth, tell us how many units share the system. That changes the plan, since a hot water outage in a six unit building is urgent in a way a single unit outage is not.

Standards and sources behind this page

In Elizabeth, as everywhere in the state, plumbing work follows the code published by the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards, and the city's construction office handles permits and inspections.
