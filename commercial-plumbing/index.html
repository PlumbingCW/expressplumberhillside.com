A backed up restrooms line, a flooded kitchen or a water heater out at a shop is a business problem as well as a plumbing one. Our emergency commercial plumbing service in Hillside is open 24/7. Close the main valve if water is spreading, call the line and tell us what the business needs to keep open.

When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

  • Sewage backing up in restrooms or a kitchen.
  • A burst pipe flooding a storefront or storage room.
  • No hot water in a restaurant or multifamily building.
  • A grease trap or line that overflows.
  • A leak above merchandise or equipment.

Kitchens, grease and floor drains

Restaurants fill their drain lines with grease faster than any home. Floor drains back up at the worst time, in the middle of a service. Hydro jetting clears the buildup and keeps lines open longer than a cable. Ask us about a cleaning schedule matched to your kitchen, so the next backup does not happen on a Friday night.

Multifamily and rental buildings

A single stack serves several units, so a clog in the basement can show up as water in an upper floor bathroom. We trace the line, clear it and tell the owner and tenant what caused it. Water heater outages and boiler trouble affect every unit, so we prioritize them. See boiler repair for heating issues.

Restrooms and backflow

Commercial restrooms need flush valves, commercial toilets and regular maintenance. Some buildings also have backflow devices that must be tested on a schedule set by the water utility or the code. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 which rules apply to your building.

Working around your hours

We schedule non-urgent work for evenings or early mornings to keep your business open. For true emergencies, we respond at any hour and tell you what to do in the first minutes: close the valve, protect stock and stop using water.

Documentation and billing

We provide clear invoices with the work done and the cause, which your insurer or landlord may ask for. Keep photos of any water damage and the date and time it started.

What to have ready when you call

  • The street address and which entrance to use.
  • Where the main water shutoff is, if you know.
  • What is backing up or leaking and since when.
  • Whether customers or tenants are affected.
  • The name of a person who can approve work.

If you are a tenant in a commercial building, tell us who the landlord is. Many repairs on shared lines need the landlord's approval, and we can contact them with you.

Small business water heaters and restrooms

A salon, a laundromat or a deli puts more demand on hot water than a household does. A tank that was right for a shop ten years ago may be too small now. We size replacements for the real use and look at recovery rate, not just the tank size. Restroom fixtures that fail inspections or leak into a ceiling below also come under this work.

A maintenance plan that prevents emergencies

Most commercial emergencies were visible a month earlier as a slow drain, a damp ceiling tile or a toilet that runs. A simple plan helps: have drain lines cleaned on a schedule, test shutoff valves, check the water heater and inspect exposed pipes before winter. We can set up visits around your hours so they do not disrupt customers.

What affects the price

  • Size and age of the building plumbing.
  • Type of work: repair, clearing or replacement.
  • Access during business hours.
  • After hours or holiday response.
  • Permits for larger work.

Standards and sources behind this page

Commercial work follows the same state plumbing code, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards, with added rules for backflow and grease control that the local office and the water utility enforce. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department which rules apply to your building.
