A planned water heater installation in Hillside lets you choose the unit instead of taking what is on the shelf. We size it to your household, set it with proper venting and connections and take the old one away. If your tank has already failed, emergency replacement is the faster route.

Choosing a type

TypeStrengthTrade off
Gas tank.Low running cost, quick recovery.Needs a vent and a gas line.
Electric tank.Simple install, no vent.Higher running cost in many homes.
Tankless.Endless hot water, small footprint.Higher install cost, gas and vent sizing.
Heat pump hybrid.Very efficient.Needs space, makes cool air.

Which one suits you depends on your fuel, your space and how many people use hot water at once. A large family with two baths used at the same time needs more recovery than a couple. Our tankless page covers those units in detail.

Sizing your heater

A tank is sized by how much hot water you use in the busiest hour. Too small and you run out mid shower. Too large and you pay to keep extra water hot. We ask how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have and whether you run a dishwasher or washer at peak.

What the install includes

  1. Site checkWe check the venting, gas line, electrical and floor drain.
  2. Remove the old unitThe old heater is drained, disconnected and carried out.
  3. Set and connectThe new unit is set on a pan where needed and connected to water, fuel and vent.
  4. Test and explainWe test safety controls and show you the shutoff and settings.

Basements and tight spaces

Many Hillside heaters sit in a basement or utility closet. Space and clearance decide what fits. A pan with a drain line protects a finished room if the tank ever fails. If the old heater sits near a sump or floor drain, we keep the new one raised and protected.

Permits and code

Water heater installs often need a permit and an inspection in New Jersey. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 about your project. We install to code, with the relief valve piped to a safe discharge and correct venting.

Expansion tanks and pressure

When water heats, it expands. In a system with a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the supply, that expansion has nowhere to go. Pressure rises and the relief valve may drip. An expansion tank gives the extra volume a place to go. We check whether your system needs one and install it if so.

Reading the energy label

Look at the first hour rating for a tank, which tells you how much hot water the unit delivers in a busy hour. Look at the energy rating for running cost. A more efficient unit costs more at the start. Over the life of the heater, the savings depend on how much hot water you use.

After installation

  • Write the install date on the tank with a marker.
  • Keep the manual and receipt together.
  • Test the relief valve once a year.
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain to clear sediment each year.

Fuel, space and the choice between repair and a new unit

A planned install is the best time to rethink the setup. If the heater sits in a damp corner of the basement, raise it on a stand. If the vent runs through a cold space, insulate it. If the gas line is long and thin, we check that it delivers enough fuel. Small changes made during an install cost far less than changes made after.

What to do before the installer arrives

  • Clear a path to the heater and a few feet around it.
  • Do not touch the gas valve or the vent if you smell gas. Leave and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Know where the water main is so it can be closed quickly.
  • Keep children and pets away from the work area.

What affects the price

  • Type, size and brand of the unit.
  • Gas line, vent or electrical work required.
  • Access and distance from the old location.
  • Expansion tank, pan or new valves.
  • Permit and inspection.

Standards and sources behind this page

Work described here falls under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 New Jersey edition, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Venting, the relief valve discharge and the gas connection are the parts inspectors check, and each follows the manufacturer's instructions as well as the code.

Efficiency choices are explained in the U.S. Department of Energy water heating guide.
