A sewer camera inspection in Hillside shows you what is inside a line you cannot see. A waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels the pipe from the cleanout to the street connection and records what it finds. You get the footage, the locator mark on the ground and a plain explanation.

The inspection is a decision tool. It tells a buyer whether a lateral is sound, tells an owner with repeat backups why, and tells a contractor where to dig before the first shovel goes in.

When to schedule an inspection

  • Before closing on a house, especially one built before 1960.
  • After two or more backups in a year.
  • When a basement floor drain gurgles or smells.
  • Before landscaping, paving or building over the line.
  • After a repair, to confirm it is clean and sloped properly.

Home purchase inspections

A standard home inspector does not usually run a camera through the sewer lateral. A failed lateral is one of the most expensive surprises in an older house. In Hillside, more than 79 percent of homes were built before 1960, so a lateral from that era deserves a look before closing. The footage can also support a request for a repair credit.

How an inspection works

  1. Find accessWe use a cleanout or pull a toilet if none exists.
  2. Run the cameraThe camera travels the full lateral while we record.
  3. Mark the lineA locator marks the depth and position of any problem at the surface.
  4. Review togetherWe show the footage, explain each finding and give you a short written summary.

What we do with a clean result

A clean line is good news and it is worth documenting. Keep the footage and the date. If a problem develops later, you will know the line was sound at that time. If the camera finds partial buildup but no damage, hydro jetting often fixes it.

What we do with a bad result

If the camera shows a failure, we explain the options and the cost for each, from a spot fix to lining to replacement, through sewer line repair. You are not obliged to hire us for the repair, and the footage is yours to share with another contractor.

Reading the footage together

We pause the video at each finding and explain it. A bright line along the pipe is a joint. A dark patch may be a root mass or a stain. A pool of water in the pipe shows a belly. If you are buying a house, share the video and the summary with your agent and your inspector, since a lateral problem is often a reason to ask for a credit.

What an inspection cannot do

A camera shows what is inside the pipe. It does not measure the condition of every foot of soil around it or predict how long a sound pipe will last. If the pipe is old but clean, we say so, and recommend a recheck in a few years.

What affects the price

  • Length of the lateral and access.
  • Whether a cleanout exists or one must be added.
  • Whether a locator mark and written summary are needed.
  • Whether it is done before a closing deadline.
  • Time of day.

Standards and sources behind this page

A camera inspection is a diagnostic step with no code approval of its own. Repairs that come out of it follow the plumbing code described by the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Digging requires a mark out through New Jersey One Call.
