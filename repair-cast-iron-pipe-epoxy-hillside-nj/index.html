Repair Cast Iron Pipe With Epoxy in Hillside, NJ

When epoxy holds, when it fails and the lasting alternatives.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

Epoxy can seal a small pinhole or hairline crack in cast iron as a temporary fix. It will not repair a pipe that is flaking, thin or badly corroded. If the pipe is decades old, the real question is how much good metal is left. Treat epoxy as a short term patch and plan the lasting repair.

Cast iron drain pipe was the standard in many houses built before the 1970s. It is durable, quiet and fireproof. It also rusts from the inside out, so a pipe that looks fine outside can be thin within.

What epoxy can and cannot do

ConditionEpoxy resultBetter choice
Small pinhole, solid pipe around it.May hold for a while.Replace the section when possible.
Hairline crack in a vertical stack.Short term hold.Cut in a new section.
Flaking, scaly interior.Will not bond well.Replace or line.
Joint leak at a hub.Often fails.Rubber coupling or replacement.
Pipe under pressure.Not suited.Replace.

How to apply an epoxy patch

  1. Turn off water useNo fixtures should drain into the pipe while you work.
  2. Clean the areaRemove rust and dirt with a wire brush and degreaser until the metal is bare.
  3. Dry it fullyEpoxy needs a dry surface to bond.
  4. Apply the epoxyMix and press it into and over the hole according to the product label.
  5. Cure and testWait the stated time before running water.

Wear gloves and ventilate the space. Follow the product's instructions closely, because cure time and temperature matter.

Signs the pipe is failing, not just leaking

  • Rust blisters or flaking on the outside.
  • A hollow sound when tapped.
  • Repeated leaks along the same run.
  • Stains on ceilings below a drain.
  • Chronic slow drains with a rough interior.

If you see these, the pipe is nearing the end of its life. Epoxy hides one weak spot while the rest of the pipe is also thin.

Lasting repair options

A plumber can cut out the bad section and replace it with PVC or new cast iron using no hub couplings. For a long run, a liner can seal the inside of the pipe. A full replacement of the stack is the most certain. A camera inspection shows the interior and helps decide. Our sewer inspection and sewer line repair pages cover the options.

When to call right away

  • Sewage is leaking into a finished space.
  • A leak is above a ceiling or electrical.
  • Several fixtures back up at once.
  • The pipe is a main stack in a multi-unit building.

How a plumber tests cast iron

A plumber taps the pipe with a hammer handle and listens. A solid ring means good metal, and a dull thud or a crunch means thin or cracked metal. A camera shows interior pitting and scale. We tell you what we see and what it means for the repair.

No hub couplings and why they are used

A no hub coupling is a rubber sleeve with a stainless band that joins two pipe ends. It lets a plumber cut out a bad section and splice in a new one without heavy work. Used with proper support, it is a lasting repair on a vertical stack and a good choice when the old pipe around it is sound.

Epoxy products: what the labels do not say

Repair putty and epoxy wraps are sold for plumbing, but most are designed for small leaks in sound pipe. They do not strengthen a thin wall, and they struggle on a surface that stays damp. Read the temperature and cure time. Cold basements slow the cure, and a patch that looks hard can still be soft underneath.

Cost thinking: patch now or replace now?

An epoxy patch costs little and buys time. If you plan to sell or renovate, a lasting repair may add value and avoid a surprise at inspection. If the pipe is old and has already leaked twice, replacing the section is the better use of money. We will give you both numbers.

Planning the replacement

If you are remodeling a bathroom or kitchen, replace the old drain while the wall is open. The extra cost is small compared with opening the wall again. Ask the plumber to document the work and photograph the new pipe before it is covered. If you are buying an older house, ask for a camera look at the stack as well as the sewer lateral.

What not to do

  • Do not use putty or tape as a permanent patch.
  • Do not rely on epoxy for a joint under pressure or movement.
  • Do not ignore a stain below a drain.
  • Do not hammer on old cast iron, which can crack it.

Standards and sources behind this page

Pipe replacement follows the plumbing code in the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards, and no hub couplings used on cast iron should be the type the manufacturer lists for that purpose. Epoxy products have their own label guidelines, so read the cure time and temperature limits.
