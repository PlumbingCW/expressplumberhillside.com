Westminster is a residential section on the western side of Hillside, close to the Elizabeth River, Conant Park and the Elizabeth city line. If you live here and have a backed-up drain, a leak or no hot water, call (908) 357-1542 and tell the dispatcher your street.

This page explains what we know about the area, what the plumbing needs of an older Hillside house look like in general, and how we handle a call from this part of town. Where the facts are uncertain, we say so.

Where Westminster is, and where its edges are unclear

Westminster is a name that local sources use, but it is not an official district with published boundaries. The Hillside school district community page describes it as the area that was home to the Pingry School, later Kean University's East Campus, and says Conant Park was established there. The Wikipedia article on Hillside also lists Westminster among the township's neighborhoods.

Real estate guides place it between the rest of Hillside and downtown Elizabeth, and they do not agree on exactly where it starts and stops. Because the edge runs along a city line, an address that people call Westminster may sit in Elizabeth. Give the dispatcher your street and the nearest cross street and we will confirm the town before we schedule. If you are across the line, our Elizabeth page covers that side.

What homes in the area look like

A real estate guide describes Westminster as mostly detached, owner-occupied houses, with colonial-style homes from the 1910s and 1920s common and some streets where houses sit close together with no side yard. That description comes from a listing site, not a survey, so treat it as a general picture.

For the township as a whole, the Union County township profile reports that more than 79 percent of housing was built before 1960. About 59 percent of homes are single-unit detached houses and another 20 percent are two-unit buildings. A building's age does not tell you what is inside the walls, so on an older house our first step is a short inspection, not a guess.

Heavy rain and basements near the river

The Elizabeth River forms the western boundary of the township, according to the 2009 Hillside master plan. The plan says an Army Corps of Engineers project was completed to protect riverside homes, and that a 2006 FEMA map removed many homes between Route 22, Bloy Street and the river from the mapped flood zone. It also lists streets that flooded in small rain events, including Conant Street under the railroad bridge. Those are notes about roads, not a list of flooded houses.

For a homeowner the practical point is simple. If your basement takes on water after a storm, check the sump pump first and the floor drain second. A pump that has never been tested may fail on the night you need it. We test the pit and the discharge, then explain whether a repair or a replacement makes sense. We do not know your property's flood history, so we do not guess at it.

Buying or owning an older house near Conant Park

Conant Park, the township's largest park, was set up in the early 1950s along the Elizabeth River and Conant Street, according to Wikipedia. The Woodruff House at 111 Conant Street dates to 1735 and is on the National Register of Historic Places. Neighborhoods with this much history tend to have a mix of building ages, and the only way to know what a particular house has is to look.

If you are buying here, a camera inspection of the sewer line before closing shows the condition of the pipe you are about to own. If you already own, ask about the age of the water heater and the pump in the basement. Both are common reasons for a call, and both are easy to check on a quiet day.
