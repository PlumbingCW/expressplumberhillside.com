If your drain keeps clogging within weeks of a cable job, emergency hydro jetting in Hillside may be the fix that holds. Jetting uses high pressure water to scour the inside of the pipe, which a cable cannot do. Call the 24/7 line and we will say whether jetting suits your line.

A cable punches a hole through the clog. A jet cleans the pipe wall. That difference is why a jetted line stays clear longer.

When a repeat clog is an emergency and jetting is the answer

  • A line that clogs again within a few weeks of cabling.
  • Heavy grease from a kitchen or a restaurant line.
  • Roots that grow back after cutting.
  • Scale or mineral buildup narrowing the pipe.
  • A main line that is slow rather than blocked.

Jetting is not right for every pipe. A cracked, collapsed or badly offset line can be damaged by pressure, which is why we run a camera first.

How hydro jetting works

  1. Camera inspectionWe look at the pipe to see its condition and the type of blockage.
  2. Choose nozzle and pressureThe nozzle and pressure are matched to the pipe material and the buildup.
  3. Jet the lineWater scours the walls, cutting roots and pushing debris to the main.
  4. Camera againWe re-inspect so you see the clean pipe and any damage found.

Grease, roots and scale

Grease cools and clings to pipe walls and builds into a thick layer that catches everything else. Roots enter at joints and fill the pipe with a fine mat. Scale builds in iron and old galvanized pipe. Jetting removes all three. Roots will return unless the opening they entered through is repaired, so we tell you if a follow up repair is needed.

Restaurants, shops and multifamily buildings

Grease lines in restaurants, shops and multifamily buildings need regular jetting to avoid backups on the busiest night. Ask us about a maintenance schedule matched to your kitchen or building. Larger systems fall under commercial plumbing.

Old pipe and jetting

Older Hillside homes often have cast iron or clay lines. Sound cast iron takes jetting well and comes out cleaner. Fragile or cracked clay needs care, and the camera tells us whether it can take the pressure. If the camera shows a failure, the next step is sewer line repair.

What to expect on the day

We set up at the cleanout and protect floors and walls around it. The camera goes in first, then the jet. Water pressure and flow vary by job, so you may hear a loud hose and see murky water at first. The line is flushed until the water runs clear. We then run the camera again so you can see the result.

After jetting: keeping the line clear

  • Keep grease out of kitchen drains and use a strainer.
  • Do not flush wipes or paper towels.
  • Have the line re-inspected if clogs return within a year.
  • Repair any joint or crack the camera found.

Jetting resets the line. How long it stays clear depends on the cause and the habits that created it.
