Route 22 is the main east and west road through Hillside and its principal commercial corridor. The 2009 Hillside master plan encourages continued regional commercial development along it, and the school district describes central industrial centers along Route 22, Interstate 78 and the railroad.

If you manage a building or run a business along this road, this page explains how we handle commercial plumbing calls. Call (908) 357-1542 and tell the dispatcher the address, the type of building and what is happening.

A corridor, not a neighborhood

This page covers a road, not a residential area. We do not describe it as a subdivision, and we do not claim to know which businesses occupy which buildings. The Wikipedia article on Hillside lists Route 22 among the major highways serving Hillside, along with Interstate 78 and the Garden State Parkway.

Heavy rain along the road

The 2009 Hillside master plan lists Route 22 West between Bloy Street and Cornell Place and Route 22 East at the Newark border among the places that flooded in small rain events. It cites undersized culverts and increased paving as causes. It also says a 2006 FEMA map removed many homes between Route 22, Bloy Street and the Elizabeth River from the mapped flood zone.

Those are notes about road drainage when the plan was written, not a record of any one building. For a building owner, the practical steps are to keep floor drains clear, test sump or ejector pumps before storms and know where the shutoff is. If your site is near the Newark line, our Newark page covers that side.

Working with owners, managers and tenants

Many commercial calls come at night when a building is empty. Tell us who has keys, where the main shutoff is and who can approve work. If the problem affects other tenants, we ask the owner or manager before we start. That keeps the job simple and avoids surprises on the invoice.
