Tankless Water Heater Temperature Settings in Hillside, NJ

The right setting, why it drifts and how to stop cold and hot swings.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

Most households do well with a tankless water heater set around 120 degrees Fahrenheit. That setting is warm enough for showers and dishes and low enough to cut scald risk. Many manufacturers and safety guides recommend it as a starting point. If your family has young children or older adults, stay at or below it.

If your water swings between cold and scalding, the setting is rarely the cause. The usual culprits are flow rate, scale or a faulty sensor. This guide shows how to tell which one you have.

How a tankless unit controls temperature

The unit senses the flow of water entering, fires the burner or the elements and measures the temperature leaving. It adjusts the heat to match the flow. When flow drops below the minimum, the unit may shut off. When flow is high, the unit may not keep up and the water cools. That is why a single low flow fixture can make the water go cold.

Step by step: set the temperature

  1. Find the controllerLook for the remote panel or the dial on the unit.
  2. Set a starting pointChoose about 120 degrees unless your manual recommends otherwise.
  3. Test at the farthest tapRun the tap for a minute and check the temperature with a thermometer.
  4. Adjust in small stepsChange by a few degrees at a time and retest.

Why flow rate matters

A tankless heater has a maximum flow it can heat. If two showers and a dishwasher run at once, the unit may reach its limit, and the water turns lukewarm everywhere. Low flow fixtures help, and a larger unit solves it if the household demand is high. If you are shopping, read the flow rating at the temperature rise you need, not the headline number.

Temperature rise and cold winter water

In winter the water entering the house is colder, so the unit must raise it further. A unit that handles three fixtures in summer may handle only two in January. This is normal, and it is one reason sizing matters. When we quote a tankless install, we size for winter supply temperatures.

Scald safety

  • Keep the set point at or below 120 degrees for homes with children or older adults.
  • Install thermostatic or anti scald valves in showers where possible.
  • Test the water before you step in or bathe a child.
  • Do not lower the set point to fix a flow issue.

If you need hotter water for a dishwasher, check the dishwasher manual. Many have a built in heater and do not need a higher household setting.

When the setting is not the problem

If the unit gives wildly uneven temperature after a flush and a power cycle, a sensor or a flow valve may be failing. A technician can test them. Our tankless water heater service covers diagnosis and repair, and if the unit has reached the end of its life, water heater installation is the next step.

Practical tips for even water

Open a tap fully when you want steady hot water, because a partly open tap may not trigger the unit. Choose showerheads with a flow rate above the unit's minimum, and consider fixtures that help rather than choke the flow. If you run a dishwasher and a shower at once, expect a dip, and stagger them during peak times.

When to call

If the unit gives scalding water even at a low set point, shut off the heater and call. That points to a sensor or a control board problem, and it is a safety issue for anyone using the water.

Showers, dishwashers and appliances together

List the hot water uses in your home that overlap: a morning shower, the dishwasher and the washing machine. A tankless unit sized for one shower may not keep up with two. If you see the issue every morning, a larger unit or a second unit for a distant bathroom can help. A plumber can calculate the flow and the temperature rise you need.

Quick checklist if the water is uneven

  • Check the set point on the controller.
  • Clean the inlet filter.
  • Open the tap fully and test again.
  • Look for an error code.
  • Schedule a flush if it has been a year.

Recirculation and wait time

If the wait for hot water at a far bathroom is long, the pipe run is the issue. A small recirculation pump or a point of use unit can shorten the wait. Some tankless models have built in recirculation. Ask a plumber whether it suits your layout.
