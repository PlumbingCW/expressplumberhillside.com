A toilet that is overflowing, leaking at the base or will not stop running needs emergency toilet repair in Hillside. If water is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, then turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise. Call the 24/7 line once the water is off.

Most toilet calls are a quick fix: a flapper, a fill valve, a wax ring or a clog. Some are a sign of a deeper line problem. The first job is to tell which one you have.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

  • Water spilling over the bowl rim and not stopping.
  • Sewage or gray water coming up in the tub when you flush.
  • A leak at the base that soaks the ceiling below.
  • A toilet that rocks, which can break the seal and let water out.
  • No other working toilet in the home.

If more than one fixture is backing up, the issue is in the line, not the toilet. See sewer line repair and drain cleaning for those cases.

Clogged toilets and overflows

A flange plunger clears most clogs in a few strokes. If two attempts fail, stop. A toy, wipe or heavy paper plug sits deeper and needs a closet auger. Forcing water through can push the clog further in and flood the floor.

A toilet that clogs every week has a reason. It can be a worn flush mechanism, a partial blockage in the trap, a vent problem or a line with a low spot. We find the reason rather than clear the same clog twice.

Leaks at the base and what they hide

A wax ring that has failed lets water seep under the toilet and into the subfloor. The damage is slow and quiet. A soft spot near the toilet or a stain on the ceiling below means the leak has been going a while. Replacing the ring is cheap. Replacing a rotted subfloor is not, so do not wait.

If water shows where you cannot trace it, leak detection can confirm whether the toilet is the source.

Toilet installation and replacement

Old toilets in older Hillside homes often use 3.5 gallons or more a flush, while a modern high efficiency toilet uses 1.28. Replacing one cuts water use and often stops chronic clogs. A new install includes setting the flange, a new seal and supply line, and a test for leaks and a full flush.

  • Standard height or comfort height bowls.
  • Round or elongated seats.
  • Rough in size, usually 12 inches from the wall, which must match the new toilet.
  • Floor condition and flange repair if the old one is corroded.

Two-family and rental buildings

Landlords can approve a toilet repair by phone, and we explain the fix to tenants. In a two-family house a stack problem can show up in both units, so we check the stack when a second floor toilet backs up into the first floor tub.

What affects the price

  • Repair of a part versus a full replacement.
  • Condition of the flange and subfloor.
  • Toilet model and rough in size.
  • Time of day, since nights and holidays cost more.
  • Whether the flange or drain line needs work.

Standards and sources behind this page

The EPA WaterSense program labels toilets that meet its flush volume and performance standards, which is a useful guide when you replace an old unit. Installation follows the plumbing code summarized by the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards.
