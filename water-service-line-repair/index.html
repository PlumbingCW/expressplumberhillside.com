If your pressure has dropped, a patch of lawn is soggy or the meter runs with every tap off, you may need emergency water service line repair in Hillside. Close the main valve inside the house and call the 24/7 line. We locate the leak and explain the repair.

The water service line is the buried pipe from the utility connection to your main shutoff. It is one of the longest pipes in a house, and the one you can see least.

When a failing service line is an emergency

  • Pressure that has dropped on every fixture.
  • A wet or sunken patch over the line.
  • A meter that moves with the house valve closed.
  • A bill that jumped with no change in use.
  • Discolored water or sediment after a repair on the street.

Who owns what

New Jersey American Water supplies Hillside, and the utility owns the main and the meter. The line from the connection to your house is generally the owner's responsibility. If you are unsure where your line starts, call the utility before you dig. Digging in a street or sidewalk also needs municipal approval, and the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 can explain permit rules.

Finding the leak

We close the house valve and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is on the buried line. Acoustic listening and a line locator then narrow the position so the excavation is small. See leak detection for how we search before we dig.

Lead and old galvanized lines

Houses built before 1960 may have a lead or galvanized steel service line. New Jersey requires water systems to find and replace lead lines, and your utility may have records for your address. If your line is lead, replacing the private side is a health decision, not only a plumbing one. We tell you what we find in the pipe.

Repair, replace or trench

A short failure in a sound line is a repair. A line that is corroded end to end is better replaced. Options include open trench and trenchless methods where the soil and layout allow. The right choice depends on the line's depth, the surface above and local approval.

Before we dig

Digging has rules. New Jersey requires a call to the one-call utility marking service before excavation, so buried gas, electric and other lines are marked. We handle that call and the permit questions. We also protect your lawn, driveway and landscaping where we can and tell you what will be disturbed.

Shutoffs, meters and what to check

  • Know where your main shutoff valve is and test it once a year.
  • Look at the meter for a moving low flow indicator.
  • Check outdoor spigots for hidden leaks.
  • Watch for unexplained wet or sunken ground over the line.

What affects the price

  • Length and depth of the line.
  • Pipe material and whether the whole run needs replacing.
  • What sits above the line: lawn, driveway or sidewalk.
  • Permits and street work.
  • Time of day.

You hear the price before work starts. If the line has failed in several places, repiping the inside of the house may be part of the plan. For your price, call (908) 357-1542.

Standards and sources behind this page

Lead service lines are being replaced across the state under a law described in the NJDEP lead service line replacement presentation, and the New Jersey One Call mark out is the standard step before digging. Repairs follow the plumbing code in the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards.
