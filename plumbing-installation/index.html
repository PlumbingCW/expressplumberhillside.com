Plumbing installation in Hillside covers the work that makes a remodel or a new fixture function: supply lines, drains, vents, shutoff valves and the final connection. We install what you choose and tell you early if the old pipes behind the wall will not support it.

What we install

  • Kitchen sinks, faucets and dishwasher hookups.
  • Bathroom vanities, tubs, showers and toilets.
  • Laundry boxes, washer drains and utility sinks.
  • Basement bathrooms and floor drains.
  • Shutoff valves, pressure regulators and hose bibs.
  • Gas appliance connections where a licensed gas fitter is required.

Planning before the wall is open

Most installation problems start before anyone picks up a tool. The drain must slope, the vent must reach, and the supply must have the capacity. A new bathroom added to a house with one half-inch supply line may give weak pressure at every fixture. We check these before you buy fixtures.

Basement bathrooms and ejector pumps

If the basement floor is below the sewer line, waste cannot flow down to it. A sealed ejector pit and pump lifts it up. This is one of the bigger jobs in a basement finish, and it needs correct venting and a permit. We explain it before the floor is cut.

Older Hillside homes

Over 79 percent of Hillside homes were built before 1960. Behind an updated bathroom there may be galvanized supply and cast iron drains from the original build. Connecting a new fixture to worn pipe is a risk. We look at what the new work connects to and tell you if a section should be replaced at the same time. See repiping if the supply is the problem.

Permits and inspections

Most installation work needs a plumbing permit in New Jersey. The Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 handles permits and inspections. We follow the plumbing code and arrange inspections, so a buyer or insurer can see the work is done properly.

Working with your contractor

We work alongside general contractors and homeowners doing their own remodel. We coordinate rough in before drywall and trim out after tile. If your project involves a failing water heater too, see water heater installation.

Choosing fixtures that will last

Fixtures are the part of a remodel you see every day, and the quality of the parts behind them decides how long they last. Solid brass valves, ceramic disc cartridges and a toilet with a proven flush mechanism cost more at the start and save repairs later. Ask what the cartridge and valve are made of, not only the finish.

What a bathroom remodel looks like for plumbing

  1. Rough inAfter demolition, we run new supply and drain lines to the planned fixture locations.
  2. InspectThe rough plumbing is tested and inspected before the walls close.
  3. Wait for finishesThe tile and drywall trades finish the room.
  4. Trim outWe set the fixtures, connect them and test every valve and drain.

Questions to ask your plumber

  • Will the permit and inspection be handled for me?
  • What are the shutoff valves and supply line materials?
  • What happens if you find old pipe behind the wall?
  • What is the warranty on the work?
  • Will you coordinate with my contractor?

Good answers to these questions mean a smoother remodel. Vague answers mean surprises.

What affects the price

  • Number and type of fixtures.
  • Access to pipes and whether walls are open.
  • Need for a new drain, vent or ejector pump.
  • Permit and inspection.
  • Whether the work is part of a larger renovation.

Standards and sources behind this page

Work described here falls under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 New Jersey edition, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards.
