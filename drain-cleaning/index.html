A kitchen sink that will not drain, a tub holding gray water, or a floor drain bubbling in the basement is a job for emergency drain cleaning in Hillside. Our 24/7 line is open nights and weekends, and a plumber can usually be scheduled the same day when one is available.

Stop running water into the clogged line as soon as you notice the problem. Every extra gallon looks for a way out, and in a basement that way out is often the floor drain. Then call, and tell us which fixtures are affected.

When a clogged drain is an emergency

A single slow sink can wait for a daytime visit. Treat it as an emergency when several fixtures back up at once, when water or sewage rises in a tub or floor drain, or when the toilet and the washing machine drain both gurgle. Those signs point to the main line, not one fixture.

  • Toilet gurgles when the washing machine drains.
  • Basement floor drain bubbles or rises during a shower.
  • Two or more fixtures clog at the same time.
  • A bad sewer smell in the lowest bathroom.
  • Water backs up into the tub when someone flushes.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen lines clog with grease, coffee grounds and food scraps that cool and harden in the trap and the horizontal run behind the wall. A plunger moves the clog a few inches. A cable machine goes past the trap and scrapes the pipe wall clean. If the sink has a disposal, we also check that the disposal outlet is not the blockage.

Hot water and dish soap do not dissolve a grease plug that is already hard. Skip chemical drain openers. They sit in a standing line, eat at old pipe and splash on whoever opens the trap next.

Tub, shower and bathroom sink clogs

Hair and soap build a slow clog just below the drain. We pull what is reachable by hand, then run a small cable through the trap arm. If the tub keeps clogging within weeks, the cause is often a vent problem or a line with a belly in it, and a camera look is the next step through sewer inspection.

Snaking, cabling and when jetting is better

A cable machine, also called snaking, drives a spinning steel cable through the line to break up or hook a clog. It is the right tool for most household blockages. When a line clogs again and again from grease, scale or roots, hydro jetting washes the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the clog.

Why older Hillside homes clog more

More than 79 percent of Hillside homes were built before 1960, and many have cast iron or clay drain lines. Cast iron gets rough on the inside as it ages, and that rough surface catches grease and paper. Clay lines have joints that roots find. A house with a 1950s drain stack and a 1990s kitchen remodel often has mismatched pipe sizes that catch debris at the transitions.

Two-family buildings add another layer. Two kitchens and two baths can feed one stack, so a clog in the basement line shows up in the upper unit first. If the second-floor tenant sees water in the tub, the cleanout in the basement is where we start.

What affects the price

You hear a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that set it:

  • Which line is blocked, a fixture or the main.
  • How far the clog sits from the access point.
  • Whether we need a cleanout cut in or can use an existing one.
  • Whether the work happens after hours.
  • Whether a camera look is added to find the cause.

If the line clogs again within weeks, a camera inspection costs less than repeated cleanings, and it shows whether you need sewer line repair instead of another round of cable work. For your price, call (908) 357-1542.

Keeping drains clear between visits

Wipe grease from pans into the trash before washing. Put a catch screen in the tub drain. Do not flush wipes, even the ones labeled flushable. Run hot water for a minute after kitchen use. If a drain slows down again, call before it stops completely, because a partial clog is a much cheaper visit.

Standards and sources behind this page

Work described here falls under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 New Jersey edition, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Cleanouts, traps and vents are part of that code, and an inspector can ask about them when work is permitted. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department if a repair goes beyond clearing a clog.
