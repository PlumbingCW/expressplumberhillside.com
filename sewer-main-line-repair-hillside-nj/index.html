Sewer Main Line Repair for Hillside, NJ Homes

Signs, diagnosis, repair options and what to ask before anyone digs.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

A failing sewer main usually shows itself with several fixtures at once: slow drains, gurgling toilets and a floor drain that bubbles. A single clogged sink is a local clog. When the pattern spreads, the main line is the suspect, and the right first step is a camera inspection.

Repair options range from a spot repair to full replacement. The cost depends on depth, material and what sits above the pipe. This guide explains the choices so you can ask good questions.

Signs the main line is failing

  • Slow drains in more than one fixture.
  • Gurgling toilets or a bubbling floor drain.
  • Sewage smell in the basement or yard.
  • Wet or sunken ground over the line.
  • Backups that return within weeks of a cleaning.

Step one: a camera inspection

Do not agree to excavate before you have seen the pipe. A camera shows whether the problem is a root clog, a cracked joint, a belly or a collapse. It also marks the depth and the spot for the dig. If a contractor wants to dig without a camera, ask why. Our sewer inspection service gives you footage you can keep.

Repair options explained

A spot repair cuts out the failed section and replaces it with new pipe. It suits a single failure in an otherwise sound pipe. Lining installs a resin liner inside the existing pipe. It avoids a trench but needs a pipe that is continuous enough to line. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old one, breaking it outward. Full open trench replacement is the most disruptive and the most certain.

Who is responsible for which part

The sewer lateral from your home to the connection at the street is generally the property owner's responsibility. The public sewer main under the street belongs to the municipality or authority. If the camera shows a failure past your property line, we explain who to call. Ask the local office about rules before any dig.

Questions to ask any contractor

  • Will you run a camera first and give me the video?
  • What are the repair options and what does each cost?
  • Is a permit needed and who pulls it?
  • What will be disturbed in my yard or driveway?
  • What is the warranty on the work?

See sewer line repair for the work we do, and drain cleaning if the problem is only a clog.

What happens to your yard

A trench repair disturbs the yard, but a good crew keeps the damage small. They lay the soil on tarps, protect nearby plants, backfill in layers and restore the surface. Ask beforehand about driveway, sidewalk or tree work. If a tree's roots caused the failure, discuss whether to remove the tree or accept recurring maintenance.

Costs: what moves the number

  • Depth and length of the line.
  • Pipe material and the repair method.
  • Surface restoration, such as concrete or paving.
  • Permits and street opening rules.
  • Access for equipment.

Get at least two written quotes that name the method, the pipe and what is restored.

After the repair: protect the investment

Ask for the final camera run, which proves the line is clear and sloped. Keep it with your house records. Add a cleanout if there is none. Avoid planting trees over the line, and keep grease and wipes out of the drains. If you ever sell, a repair record and a clean camera video are a selling point.

Shared laterals and neighbors

In some neighborhoods two houses share a lateral. If a failure is in the shared section, both owners may have a stake. Talk to the neighbor early, share the camera footage and agree who pays for what in writing. We can explain what we found, but cost sharing is between you and your neighbor.

What to know in Hillside

Per Union County's township profile, more than 79 percent of Hillside homes were built before 1960. Laterals from that period are often clay or cast iron, so a camera inspection before a purchase makes sense.

Call the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 before excavating. They can tell you what permit and inspection a sewer repair needs and how work near a street or sidewalk is handled.

Sewer backup damage is often excluded from homeowners policies unless you carry an endorsement. Ask your insurer before you need it.

Neighbors we serve

We repair sewer laterals in Elizabeth and Maplewood as well, where old clay and cast iron lines and tree roots are common.

Planning ahead

If your house is older and has never had a camera inspection, schedule one before it becomes an emergency. A failing sewer is much cheaper to plan than to repair at midnight with sewage in the basement. Add a cleanout if the house lacks one, and keep the footage for your records.

How long a repair lasts

A new PVC lateral can last for decades. A lining has a long life on a sound host pipe. A spot repair lasts as long as the rest of the pipe holds up. Ask the contractor what lifespan they expect and what the warranty covers. If the neighboring pipe is old, the next failure may be close by.

Standards and sources behind this page

Sewer laterals fall under the state plumbing code, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Before anyone digs, the New Jersey One Call mark out is the standard step that locates buried gas, electric and water lines.
