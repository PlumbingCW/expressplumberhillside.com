Repair a Leaky Outside Faucet in Hillside, NJ

Stop the drip at the handle or spout and prevent winter damage inside the wall.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

A leaky outside faucet is usually a worn washer or packing nut, and fixing it takes about twenty minutes. The bigger risk is not the drip. It is water freezing in the pipe behind the wall in winter. Fix the leak and disconnect the hose before the first cold night.

If water is leaking inside the house near the faucet, shut off the main valve and call. A burst pipe behind an exterior wall is an emergency. See burst pipe repair.

Where is it leaking?

Where the water appearsLikely causeFix
From the spout when closed.Worn washer or seat.Replace the washer.
Around the handle stem.Packing nut or O ring loose.Tighten or replace packing.
Where the hose connects.Worn hose washer.Replace the hose washer.
Inside the house near the wall.Frozen or split pipe behind the bib.Shut off water and call.

Step by step: replace a washer

  1. Shut off the waterClose the indoor shutoff valve for the outdoor line if you have one.
  2. Open the faucetDrain the line and relieve pressure.
  3. Remove the handle and stemUnscrew the handle, then the packing nut, and pull out the stem.
  4. Replace the washerTake off the old washer and fit an exact match.
  5. Reassemble and testPut everything back and test with the water on.

Frost-free faucets

A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that puts the shutoff inside the heated part of the house. When it is closed, the water stops warm, so there is nothing to freeze in the exposed pipe. It must slope slightly downward so the pipe drains. If the hose stays attached, water can stay in the barrel and freeze anyway. Always remove hoses in autumn.

How to winterize any outdoor faucet

  • Disconnect and drain hoses and splitters.
  • Close the indoor shutoff for the line, if there is one.
  • Open the outdoor faucet to drain what is left.
  • Cover the faucet with an insulated dome.
  • Leave the faucet open a little all winter if you have an indoor shutoff.

Spotting freeze damage

A bib that froze may not leak until it thaws. If you see water running down a wall, a stain below the bib or hear dripping in the wall after a cold snap, the pipe has probably split. Turn off the water and call. If you wait, the water can soak insulation and drywall.

If the pipe has to be replaced, we usually fit a frost-free bib at the same time. See faucet repair for our service.

Choosing a replacement bib

Pick a frost-free bib with a stem length that matches the wall thickness, and a built in vacuum breaker. The vacuum breaker prevents hose water from flowing back into the house supply. Choose solid brass for durability. If the wall is brick, the bib may need a specific length. A plumber can measure and fit it correctly.

Garden hoses and pressure

Leaving a hose attached under pressure strains the faucet and the hose washer. Close the faucet at the end of the day and disconnect long hoses in the cold season. A short, quick disconnect at the faucet makes this easier.

Check it each spring

When you reconnect the hose in spring, open the faucet slowly and look inside at the wall near the pipe. A drip or stain after the first use can mean winter damage. Catching it early prevents rot in the wall.

Tools and parts for the job

  • An adjustable wrench and a screwdriver.
  • Replacement washers and packing in the right size.
  • Plumber grease and thread tape.
  • A bucket and a rag.
  • A flashlight for the indoor shutoff.

Take the old stem to the hardware store to match the parts. Outdoor faucets come in several sizes, and a wrong washer will leak again.

Signs the faucet needs replacing

If the stem is corroded, the handle spins without turning the valve or the body has a crack, replace the whole faucet. A pitted seat that will not seal with a new washer also means replacement. A frost-free bib replacement takes a plumber about an hour in a typical wall, and it prevents the winter problem for good.

Water on the bill

A hose bib that drips all summer wastes water and money. A drop per second adds up to thousands of gallons a year. If you notice a higher water bill with no change in use, check outdoor faucets and the meter. If you cannot find the source, leak detection can trace it.

When to call a plumber

  • The shutoff valve is stuck or broken.
  • Water appears inside the house.
  • The faucet is not frost-free and the pipe is exposed.
  • The stem is corroded and will not come out.
  • You want the bib replaced with a frost-free model.

Standards and sources behind this page

Frost-free bibs and vacuum breakers are specified by plumbing code, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. The EPA WaterSense program also publishes guidance on finding leaks around the home.
