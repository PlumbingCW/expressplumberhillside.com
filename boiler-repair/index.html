Hot water and steam systems

A hot water boiler pumps heated water through baseboards or radiators. A steam boiler boils water and sends the steam up pipes. Older Hillside homes often have steam or hot water systems from the original build, and they behave differently. We work on both and explain what is wrong in plain terms.

Carbon monoxide and safety

A boiler burns fuel and needs venting. Carbon monoxide is odorless, so keep a working CO detector on every level. If the alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911. A yellow or lazy burner flame, soot around the boiler or a pilot that keeps going out are warning signs. Do not use the boiler until it is checked.

Annual service

A boiler checked every year is less likely to fail in the coldest week. Service includes cleaning the burner, testing safety controls, checking pressure and examining the flue. It is much cheaper than an emergency.

Repair or replace

Many boilers last decades. If the heat exchanger has cracked or repairs are frequent and costly, replacement may make sense. We explain the cost of each and let you decide. For hot water supply problems see water heater repair.

Permits and code

Boiler replacement normally needs a permit and an inspection. Ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100.

Signs your boiler is near the end of its life

  • Repairs are needed every winter and the parts bill is growing.
  • The unit is rusty, leaking at the body or has a cracked heat exchanger.
  • Rooms heat unevenly even after the system is bled.
  • Fuel bills rise with no change in use.
  • The boiler is noisy, with banging, whistling or rumbling.

A boiler that fits two or three of these is a candidate for replacement planning. We would rather help you plan than send you an emergency bill in January.

Radiators, baseboard and air in the lines

A radiator that is warm at the bottom and cold at the top has trapped air. Hot water systems have a small bleed valve at the top of each radiator. Open it slightly with the system running until water spits, then close it. If the radiator stays cold, the system pressure may be low or a valve may be stuck.

Steam radiators work differently. A steam radiator that hisses and stays cold may have a stuck air vent. Do not close the supply valve halfway, because partial closure makes a steam radiator knock. It should be fully open or fully closed.

Before the heating season

  1. Check the pressureMany residential hot water boilers sit near 12 psi when cold. Check your manual for your model.
  2. Look at the flameA steady blue flame is normal. Yellow or lazy flames mean trouble.
  3. Test the CO alarmsReplace batteries and make sure every level has a working alarm.
  4. Book a service visitA yearly cleaning and safety check is far cheaper than an emergency.

What to tell us when you call

Tell the dispatcher the fuel, the boiler type, any lights or codes on the unit and what you have already tried. The more detail you give, the more likely the right part is on the truck.
