A tankless water heater gives hot water on demand without a storage tank, and it needs the right service to keep working. We handle tankless water heater installation, repair and flushing in Hillside, from a cold water sandwich to an error code that will not clear.

How tankless units differ

A tankless unit heats water as it flows. There is no tank to fail or run out, but the unit needs enough gas or electric power to heat a full flow rate. A gas tankless often needs a larger gas line and special venting. An electric one needs a heavy circuit.

Common tankless problems

  • Error code that stops the unit.
  • Water that runs cold, then hot, then cold.
  • Reduced flow from scale in the heat exchanger.
  • Unit that shuts off during use.
  • Noise, popping or banging on startup.

Descaling and annual flushing

Hard water leaves scale inside the heat exchanger and cuts flow and efficiency. Most manufacturers call for a vinegar or descaling flush at least once a year in hard water areas. Check your local water quality report from New Jersey American Water or test your tap, since we do not assume your hardness.

Installation and conversion

Converting from a tank to tankless is more than swapping boxes. We check gas pipe size, venting, condensate drainage, electrical and the space on the wall. If the home cannot support a tankless unit, we will say so and suggest a tank or hybrid through water heater installation.

Tankless repair or replacement

Many tankless failures are fixable: a flow sensor, an igniter or a clogged exchanger. If the unit is old and the heat exchanger itself has failed, replacement may be better. A traditional tank problem belongs on the water heater repair page.

Is tankless worth it for a Hillside house?

Tankless heaters cost more to install and use less space. They save by not heating stored water when nobody needs it. How much that saves depends on your use. A small household with low demand may save little, while a large family that runs several showers will value the steady supply. We give both numbers before you decide.

Gas, venting and the basement

A gas tankless unit burns a lot of fuel at once, so the gas line must be sized to supply it. Many older homes have a line that suits a tank and must be upsized. Venting is also different, because high efficiency units use plastic venting and a condensate drain. In a basement, we check where the vent can exit and where the condensate can drain.

Maintenance that keeps the warranty

Keep records of annual flushes. Manufacturers may ask for them if there is a warranty claim. A short log with the date and what was done is enough.
