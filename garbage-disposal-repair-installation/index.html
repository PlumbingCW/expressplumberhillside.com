A disposal that hums and will not turn, leaks into the cabinet or backs up the sink needs emergency garbage disposal repair in Hillside. Switch it off at the wall and never put a hand into the chamber. Then call the 24/7 line.

Disposal problems rarely come alone. A jam, a leak and a backed up sink often share a cause: the grinder, the drain line behind it or the dishwasher connection.

When a disposal problem is an emergency

  • Water leaking from the bottom or sides into the cabinet.
  • Sink backing up while the unit runs.
  • Smoke or a burning smell.
  • The unit tripping a breaker every time it starts.

Jams and humming

A humming disposal with no spin is usually jammed. With the power off, many units have a hex socket at the bottom for a wrench that frees the flywheel. A reset button on the bottom restarts a tripped motor. If neither works, the motor or the grinder may have failed.

Backups, clogs and the drain behind it

A disposal grinds food, but it does not move it down the line. Grease and coffee grounds settle in the pipe behind it and form a plug. If the sink clogs again after the unit is cleared, the line needs work through drain cleaning rather than another disposal repair.

Repair versus replacement

Units commonly last around a decade. A leak from the body is a replacement. A jam or a tripped reset is usually a repair. A unit that is loud, slow and old often costs more to repair than to swap.

Installing a new disposal

  1. Power off and disconnectWe shut off power and take off the old unit and mount.
  2. Mount the new unitThe mounting ring and flange are set with a proper seal.
  3. Connect drain and dishwasherDrain, dishwasher knockout and wiring are connected.
  4. Test for leaksWe run water and the unit and check every joint.

What not to put in a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity.
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell.
  • Anything that is not food.

Run cold water before, during and after use. Cold keeps grease solid so it is chopped rather than smeared on the pipe.

Electrical and switch considerations

A disposal runs on a dedicated switch or on a plug into an outlet under the sink. If the switch is bad or the outlet is not grounded, the disposal will trip or fail to start. We check the power before we blame the unit. Any wiring change that needs a licensed electrician is something we tell you at the start.

Dishwasher connections

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a port on the disposal. New units have a plug inside this port that must be knocked out. If it is left in, the dishwasher cannot drain and water stands in the machine. The hose should also loop high under the counter to keep disposal water out of the dishwasher.

When the smell is the problem

Odor usually comes from food trapped under the splash guard. Scrub it with a brush and dish soap, then run ice and a little citrus peel. If the smell continues, grease is coating the drain line behind the unit, and the line needs cleaning.

What affects the price

  • Repair of a jam versus a new unit.
  • Horsepower and model of the replacement.
  • Electrical work if the outlet or switch needs changes.
  • Condition of the drain and dishwasher connection.
  • Time of day.

Disposal installs involve plumbing and electrical work. The plumbing side follows the code described by the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Any wiring should follow the electrical rules your local office enforces.
