When the hot water stops or a tank starts leaking, you need emergency water heater repair in Hillside, not a long wait. Our 24/7 line is open nights and weekends. If water is on the floor, turn off the cold water valve above the tank and the power or gas first, then call.

Many water heater calls come in on the coldest mornings, because a unit that has been limping for months finally quits when demand is highest. A plumber can often diagnose it on the first visit and fix it the same day when the part is available.

When a water heater problem is an emergency

Call at once if water is leaking from the tank, if you smell gas, or if the relief valve is discharging hot water. For a gas smell, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. A tank leaking at the bottom seam usually cannot be repaired and points to water heater replacement.

No hot water alone is urgent but rarely dangerous. In winter, a house without hot water is a house with a problem, and most causes are quick to fix.

Gas water heater problems

  • Pilot light will not stay lit, often a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot tube.
  • Burner lights but the water stays lukewarm, often sediment in the tank bottom or a failing gas valve.
  • Rumbling or popping noises from hardened sediment.
  • Rusty hot water, which can point to a failing anode rod or tank corrosion.
  • Draft or venting trouble that makes the unit shut off.

Electric water heater problems

  • No hot water at all, often a tripped high-limit reset or a failed upper element.
  • Water that runs out fast, which points to a failed lower element.
  • Breaker that keeps tripping, which can mean a shorted element.
  • Leaking at the element gasket, which is a repair rather than a replacement.

Tank leaks, relief valves and expansion tanks

Not every wet floor means a dead tank. A loose drain valve, a failed relief valve or a leaking supply connection looks alike at first. We dry the area, trace the water to its source and tell you plainly whether the repair is worth it. If the tank itself has failed, we will say so rather than sell you a repair that will not last.

Closed plumbing systems, common where a backflow device sits on the water service, can push pressure above safe limits when the heater runs. An expansion tank gives that extra volume somewhere to go. If your relief valve drips every morning, that is a reason to ask about one.

Electric, gas and hybrid units

Hybrid and heat pump water heaters are newer and have their own faults, such as condensate drains and fan trouble. We repair what we can and are honest about when a repair no longer makes sense. If you have a tankless unit, see tankless water heater service instead.

Why age matters in Hillside homes

Most tank heaters last about a decade, and many in older Hillside basements are well past that. A unit near a floor drain or a sump may be the one that finally floods the room. If your heater is more than ten years old and starts needing repairs, compare the repair price with replacement before you spend on parts.

Hard water shortens the life of any heater by building scale on the elements and the tank floor. Ask New Jersey American Water for your latest water quality report, or test your tap, before you decide on a softener. We do not guess at your hardness.

What affects the repair price

  • Gas or electric, and the part that failed.
  • Whether the unit is easy to reach in a basement or tight closet.
  • Whether venting, gas lines or a permit are involved.
  • Time of day, since nights and holidays cost more.
  • Whether the tank is repairable or needs replacing.

Permit rules differ by job, so ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 if you are unsure. For your price, call (908) 357-1542 before any work begins.

Standards and sources behind this page

Work described here falls under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 New Jersey edition, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards. Gas and electric tanks are covered by that code, and the CDC carbon monoxide guidance explains why every level of a home should have a working alarm when fuel is burned indoors.

For general guidance on running costs and sizing, see the U.S. Department of Energy water heating guide. Our visits follow the manufacturer's instructions for each unit.
