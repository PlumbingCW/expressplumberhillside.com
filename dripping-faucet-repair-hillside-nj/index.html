Dripping Faucet Repair for Hillside, NJ Homes

Which part has worn out, what you can fix with a screwdriver and when to call.

By the Hillside Express Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026.

☎ Call (908) 357-1542

A dripping faucet is almost always a worn part inside the handle, not a bad faucet. In most cases you can stop the drip in under an hour with a few basic tools. First turn off the shutoff valves under the sink, then work out which of the four common faucet types you have.

If the faucet will not shut off at all, or water is spraying under the sink, skip the repair guide and call a plumber. A fast leak needs emergency faucet repair rather than a weekend project.

Why a faucet drips

Every drip comes from water getting past a seal that has worn. The seal can be a rubber washer, a ceramic disc, a cartridge or a valve seat. Hard water speeds the wear, because mineral scale roughens the surfaces the seal presses on. In a house with older pipes, sediment can also scratch the valve seat.

A drip of one drop a second wastes thousands of gallons a year. That is money on your water bill and wear on the fixture, so it is worth the hour.

The four faucet types and what wears out

TypeHow to spot itWhat usually wears
Compression.Two handles, you turn them several times.Rubber washer and valve seat.
Cartridge.One or two handles, smooth motion.Cartridge or its O rings.
Ball.Single handle over a rounded cap.Springs, seats and the cam.
Ceramic disc.Single handle, short quarter turn.Neoprene seals or the disc cylinder.

Step by step: fix a dripping cartridge or compression faucet

  1. Shut off the waterClose both valves under the sink, then open the faucet to drain it.
  2. Plug the drainPut a rag in the drain so a small screw cannot fall in.
  3. Remove the handlePry off the cap, take out the screw and lift the handle.
  4. Pull the worn partUnscrew the packing nut and take out the cartridge or the stem and washer.
  5. Replace and reassembleMatch the new part exactly, add a thin film of plumber grease and reassemble in reverse.
  6. TestOpen the shutoff valves slowly and check for drips at the handle and spout.

Take the old part to the hardware store so the new one matches. A near fit leaks again within weeks.

When the drip is not the faucet

If the drip is under the sink, the cause is probably a supply line or a drain connection. Braided supply lines wear out in about ten years and fail without a warning. A leak at the base of the faucet is an O ring. A leak from a pipe in the cabinet that you cannot trace may need leak detection to find.

When to call a plumber instead

  • The shutoff valves under the sink are stuck, corroded or missing.
  • The faucet is old and parts are no longer sold.
  • The valve seat is pitted and the new washer will not seal.
  • Water is hitting the cabinet floor or a ceiling below.
  • You fixed it twice and it still drips.

A plumber carries common cartridges and can replace the whole faucet when repair is not worth it. The honest answer is sometimes a new faucet, and we will say so with the price of each.

Tools and parts to gather first

  • An adjustable wrench and a set of screwdrivers.
  • A hex key set, since many handles use small set screws.
  • Plumber grease and replacement O rings.
  • A small container so no screw is lost.
  • A flashlight for the dark space under the sink.

Photograph each step as you go. The pictures help you reassemble the handle in the right order. Most mistakes happen when parts are put back backwards.

How to tell cartridge and compression faucets apart

Turn the handle. If it moves smoothly in a short arc, it is probably a cartridge or ceramic disc. If you have to turn it several times and it gets tighter as it closes, it is a compression faucet. This matters because the repair parts are different, and buying the wrong one is the most common mistake.

If the leak is at the handle or the base

A drip at the base of the spout usually means worn O rings. Unscrew the spout, take off the old rings, coat the new ones with plumber grease and fit them. If water seeps out around the handle, the packing or the cartridge cap is loose. Tighten gently, because overtightening can crack the body.

What a drip costs you

Count the drips for a minute to estimate the loss. Ten drips a minute adds up to a few gallons a day. Over a year the cost on a water and sewer bill is real, and the constant moisture stains the sink and promotes mineral buildup. A small repair now is cheaper than a stained fixture later.

Keeping faucets from dripping again

  • Close faucet handles gently. Cranking them wears the seal.
  • Clean the aerator every few months to clear scale.
  • Replace braided supply lines about every ten years.
  • Use the shutoff valves once a year so they keep working.
