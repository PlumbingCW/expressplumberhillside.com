A tank that is leaking from the bottom is finished, and emergency water heater replacement in Hillside is the fix. Turn off the cold supply and the gas or power, then call the 24/7 line. We can often get a new unit installed the same day when one is available.

The goal is a short wait, not a long outage. We arrive with a plan for your space, carry the old tank out, and leave the new one connected, tested and venting correctly.

When a water heater needs replacing right now

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank.
  • A visible crack or rust streak near the bottom seam.
  • A tank older than about ten years that now needs frequent repairs.
  • Rust colored hot water that does not clear.
  • A repair quote that is close to the price of a new unit.

If you are not sure whether the tank is repairable, water heater repair diagnosis comes first. We will say plainly when a repair is not worth it.

Choosing the right replacement

The unit you pick should match your household, your fuel and your space. A family of five needs more storage than two adults. A gas unit needs a safe vent path. Electric needs the right circuit. Tankless saves floor space but needs correct gas sizing and venting, which is why tankless service has its own page.

What a replacement visit includes

  1. Shut off and drainWe close the water and fuel, drain the old tank and disconnect it.
  2. Remove and haul awayThe old tank is carried out and taken away.
  3. Set the new unitWe level it, add a pan or drain line where needed and connect water lines.

  5. Test and explainWe bring it to temperature, check the relief valve and show you the shutoff.

Basements, floor drains and old Hillside plumbing

Most Hillside tank heaters sit in a basement or utility closet. That is good news for access, but it also means a failing tank sends water to a floor drain or a finished room. Moving a heater onto a drain pan with a drain line is a cheap way to protect the next one.

Older supply lines may be galvanized or mixed with copper. When we replace the heater, we check those connections and tell you if they are corroded. Swapping a heater onto a worn line only moves the leak to the next fitting.

Permits, venting and code

Replacing a water heater can need a permit in New Jersey, and the rules vary by town, so ask the Hillside Building and Housing Department at (973) 926-5100 before you schedule. The department lists a plumbing subcode official for such questions. Proper venting, a relief valve piped to a safe place and clearance around the unit all matter for safety.

What affects the replacement price

  • Unit type, size and fuel.
  • Condition of the vent, gas line, valves and drain.
  • Access to the install location.
  • Whether an expansion tank or pan is needed.
  • Whether the visit happens after hours.

You hear the price before work starts. Planned swaps for a unit that still works are covered by water heater installation instead. For your number, call (908) 357-1542.

After the install

Set the thermostat no higher than needed for your household, check the relief valve discharge pipe for drips, and flush a tank unit once a year to clear sediment. Keep the paperwork so you know the install date when warranty questions come up.

Standards and sources behind this page

Work described here falls under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 New Jersey edition, according to the New Jersey Division of Codes and Standards.
